## Confidence

Config `1.0.0`. Six weighted components summing to 1.0, scaled by a contradiction multiplier, then mapped to a band and a calibration lookup.

| Component | Weight |
|---|---:|
| contribution | 0.25 |
| counterfactual | 0.15 |
| data_quality | 0.15 |
| evidence_strength | 0.20 |
| robustness | 0.20 |
| temporal_precedence | 0.05 |

Part 13.4 weights `(1 - p_value)` at 0.20. That p-value is the Welch test on the PELT-selected window, which ADR-017 showed is a post-selection statistic reading p < 0.001 on pure noise. Bootstrap robustness replaces it; the Welch value is carried as diagnostic metadata and scores nothing.

## Calibration

**Synthetic.** Seeded from 64 labelled cases in the ground-truth run over the generated dataset. **Not production history.**

| Band | Correct | Total | Observed | Used in arithmetic | Reportable |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|:--:|
| HIGH | 12 | 12 | 100% | 0.929 | yes |
| INSUFFICIENT | 34 | 49 | 69% | 0.686 | yes |
| LOW | 0 | 1 | 0% | 0.333 | no - UNCALIBRATED |
| MEDIUM | 1 | 2 | 50% | 0.500 | no - UNCALIBRATED |

The two accuracy columns differ on purpose. The observed rate is what was seen and is what a reader is shown. The arithmetic uses a Laplace-smoothed estimate, because 12 correct out of 12 is not evidence of a 100% success rate — it is evidence that no failure has been observed yet, and feeding 1.0 into `(1 - p_model) x cost` makes the model's expected loss identically zero at every decision value, which silently turns the cost-sensitive rule into 'always automate'.

Bands below 10 observed cases report `UNCALIBRATED` rather than a quoted rate. On this seed that is MEDIUM and LOW — the system says it does not know how often a medium call has been right, because it genuinely does not.

## Deferral

Policy `1.0.0`. The rule is not `confidence < 0.7 -> human`:

```
E[loss | model] = (1 - p_model) x cost_of_error
E[loss | human] = (1 - p_human) x cost_of_error + review_cost
defer  <=>  E[loss | model] >= E[loss | human]
```

Review cost is analyst time (3,500 INR/h x 1.5 h) plus the delay cost on a live incident (12,000 INR/h x 4 h).

These guardrails override the arithmetic, because expected-loss reasoning is sound on average and these are the cases where the average is the wrong frame:

- **never automated**: `L_CHECKOUT_ROLLBACK`, `L_PRICING_REVIEW` — the asymmetry between a wrong action and a delayed one is not captured by one cost-of-error figure
- **no rights, no automation** (`require_persona_any_rights: true`): a persona with neither approval nor request rights on a lever never has it automated on their behalf. Request rights automate the *request*; only approval rights would automate the action
- **at or below `INSUFFICIENT` the system abstains** rather than deferring: there is nothing for a reviewer to review
- **UNCALIBRATED always defers**: with no observed hit rate there is no `p_model` to put in the comparison


### What "automate" is permitted to mean

`automate` is not a single privilege. It is bounded by the persona's rights on the specific lever, and the bound is recorded on the decision as `automation_scope`:

| Scope | The system may | The system may not |
|---|---|---|
| `raise_request` | raise the request with the owning role | perform the remediation |
| `execute` | take the action itself | act outside the lever's `action_template` |
| `none` | nothing | anything |

For `L_GATEWAY_ESCALATE` specifically, every persona in this system (`ops_lead`, `analytics_lead`, `finance_director`) holds **request** rights only - `can_approve` is `[engineering_lead, cto]`, and neither is a persona the system runs as. So the automated act is *raising an escalation with Engineering*. Rolling back the release is a different lever, `L_CHECKOUT_ROLLBACK`, and it is unreachable by automation through three independent guards: it is on the `never_automate_lever_ids` list, no persona can approve it, and it requires a licensed causal claim. **No lever in this catalogue authorises the system to execute a technical remediation.** Permitting one would require a new lever, reviewed as such.

## Abstention states

| State | Remedy |
|---|---|
| `no_material_event` | nothing to do: no movement cleared the materiality gate |
| `sparse_history` | wait for history; this slice needs more observations before a seasonal baseline exists |
| `evidence_insufficiency` | a movement is real but nothing corroborates an explanation; name the missing source and the gap closes |
| `conflicting_evidence` | two explanations are equally supported; a human decides which, and they imply different owners |
| `unauthorized_information` | the evidence needed is not readable by this role; a permitted reader can answer it |
| `unsupported_causal_claim` | the association holds but causation was not established; the finding stands, the wording must stay associative |
| `multi_dimensional` | the cause spans a combination of dimensions, which this system does not localise |

Six states rather than one, because each has a different remedy. A reader told *why* the system declined can act; a reader told only *that* it declined cannot.

## Feedback routing

| Outcome | Artifact updated | Timing | Visible effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| `accepted` | `config/calibration.json :: calibration_events` | live | the reliability text on the confidence chip changes |
| `rejected` | `eval/attribution_labels.jsonl and config/scoring.yaml` | batched | hypothesis ordering changes on future similar cases |
| `corrected` | `eval/narrative_corrections.jsonl and prompts/*.txt` | batched | fewer Gate 2 violations of that type |
| `escalated` | `config/deferral.yaml :: human_accuracy` | live | the deferral rule's routing shifts for that bucket |
| `insufficient_evidence` | `coverage_gaps register` | batched | Gate 1 abstains earlier and more specifically |

Two outcomes update live because both are counters and neither needs a model. The other three accumulate and are applied on human review. No fine-tuning, no auto-applied prompt changes, no unbounded weight drift — saying so is a credibility gain rather than a gap.

## Limitations

- **The calibration set is synthetic and small** (64 cases, 2 band(s) above the reporting floor). It measures this system on this generated dataset and says nothing about production accuracy.
- **HIGH at 12/12 is not a 100% success rate.** It is a small sample with no observed failure, which is why the arithmetic smooths it and why the displayed text shows the counts.
- **`p_human` is seeded, not measured.** The values in `config/deferral.yaml` are estimates that the `escalated` feedback outcome would refine; until analysts have resolved real escalations, the human arm of the comparison rests on an assumption.
- **Decision values are illustrative.** The rupee figures for what a wrong call costs each persona are plausible placeholders, not measured business impact, and they move the automate/defer boundary directly.
- **S6 is not an independent entitlement test.** S5a and S6 run the same KPI, slice, window and persona, so they produce identical decisions by construction. The CRM note is withheld in both, because withholding is a property of the *role*, not of the scenario - which is the correct behaviour, but it means S6 demonstrates nothing that S5a does not. The contrast worth reading is S5a/S6 (`ops_lead`, 1 item withheld) against S1 and S5b (`analytics_lead`, `finance_director`, nothing withheld) on the same underlying event.
- **Elasticity impact is a fraction, not a fitted model.** `elasticity_estimate` currently applies a configured recovery fraction to the measured movement rather than fitting a real price-response curve; the range is wider to reflect that, and the method is named in the output.
